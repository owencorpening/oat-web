I built an interactive writing systems timeline

April 13, 2026 · Vectors

← PreviousNext →

I built an interactive writing systems timeline — color-coded by region, zoomable, font-adjustable, and it works on mobile. Zero infrastructure: one HTML file, GitHub Pages, free forever.

It started when I screenshot a slide from a presentation — five events, no sources, small text. I asked Claude to rebuild it with scholarly references, better contrast, and font controls for folks with low vision. An hour later it had 40 entries across 7 civilizations with peer-reviewed citations on every dot.

What I didn't know before I started:

— The Greek alphabet is only ~2,800 years old. Before that, Greek went completely unwritten for 400 years after the Bronze Age collapse wiped out Linear B.

— Vedic Sanskrit — the Rigveda — was composed around 1500 BCE and transmitted orally with extraordinary precision for over a thousand years before anyone wrote it down.

— Every alphabet on Earth — Latin, Arabic, Hebrew, Greek, Devanagari — descends from a single consonantal script scratched by Semitic workers in a Sinai turquoise mine around 1850 BCE.

— Michael Ventris, the architect who deciphered Linear B in 1952, was not a professional linguist. He cracked a 3,000-year-old script as a side project.

Click around. Filter by region. Zoom in on the Bronze Age. It holds up on a phone.

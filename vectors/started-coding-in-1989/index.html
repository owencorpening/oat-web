I Started Coding in 1989. In 2022, Tech Disappeared.

October 5, 2025 · Vectors

Introduction

I began my full-time software development career in 1989. Before that, I was a civil engineer — licensed, experienced, and grounded in real-world infrastructure. Over the decades, I transitioned fully into software, eventually becoming a senior engineer at multiple companies. I've seen multiple tech cycles, booms and busts, and watched the internet evolve from SGML and FTP to real-time APIs and cloud services.

But 2022 wasn't another bust. It wasn't cyclical. It was a collapse — quiet, almost invisible in real time, but absolute in hindsight.

What Used to Happen

In earlier downturns — dot-com 2000, financial crisis 2008 — companies folded, people regrouped, and a new wave of innovation soon followed. Founders reemerged. Startups rose from the ashes. The machinery of risk and renewal still functioned.

What Happened in 2022

That didn't happen this time.

  • Venture capital dried up — not paused, but vanished.
  • There were no IPOs.
  • Entire startup pipelines disappeared.
  • Rooftop launch parties and innovation hubs went quiet.
  • Even the culture of technical experimentation — a hallmark of the craft — faded.

It wasn't just a funding problem. The people left. Developers, designers, ops folks, recruiters — moved on to AI, government work, trades, or caregiving. Many of them never came back.

I Was There

I saw it firsthand:

  • Companies I worked at were acquired and absorbed. Gone.
  • I have thousands of LinkedIn connections, but the startup chatter is gone.
  • I worked with email infrastructure and requirements management. Those worlds are silent now.
  • I spent decades building things — and now, no one seems to be building.

What This Means

The collapse was quiet because there was no crash — only evaporation.

A generation of engineers now faces:

  • A hiring landscape skewed toward short-term contracting or platform maintenance.
  • Fewer mentors. Fewer new startups. Almost no junior positions.
  • Age bias that locks out experienced builders.

Where I Went

I didn't quit. I still write software. I use Git and Markdown to manage writing. I use VS Code. I still care about process, versioning, clarity.

And I think that's worth saying out loud: I didn't leave. I'm still here, still building — even if it's not on a VC-funded rocket ship.

What's Next?

I don't have a solution, just a report from the front. But if you've been wondering where the startups went, or why the hiring pool feels drained, or why job posts are ghost towns — you're not imagining things.

I plan to keep writing — maybe here on Substack, maybe in Markdown for now — about what happened, how we adapt, and what survives.

If you're still here too, I'd love to hear from you.

We Don’t Have a Climate Problem; We Have a Mass-Balance Problem

January 17, 2026 · Vectors

← PreviousNext →

As a Systems Engineer—with a career spanning from highway design at Caltrans and TxDOT to enterprise infrastructure at IBM and Travelers—I look at the global water crisis through the lens of inputs and outputs.

Currently, our global hydrological system is "overdrawn." We are withdrawing more from our aquifers than we are depositing. This scarcity exists independently of any climate debate; it is a straightforward engineering failure to maintain our primary life-support infrastructure.

The Calculation:

By moving desalinated water inland at scale, we can restore depleted aquifers and stabilize river/lake levels. A notable byproduct: every 1,000 km³ moved inland lowers global sea level by about 2.8 mm.

The Blueprint:

🔹 The Artery: Multi-use, lit, and secured corridors for water, power, and road/rail traffic.

🔹The Engine: Standardized, mass-produced SMR-Desalination Units.

🔹 The Metric: Cutting into the roughly 400,000 under-five deaths a year from waterborne disease, at a low cost per life saved.

This isn't a "vision" for a better world; it’s a Requirement Document for a functional one.

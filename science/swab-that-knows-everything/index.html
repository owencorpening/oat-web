The Swab That Knows Everything

May 23, 2026 · Science

Following up on my post about the Oxford Nanopore MinION.

Here's the scenario I keep thinking about.

Nurse swabs your throat. Right now this is what happens next:

  • Rapid strep test: yes/no, no strain info
  • Flu swab: A or B, nothing more
  • "Viral vs bacterial" is often a clinical guess
  • Culture results: 24–72 hours
  • You leave with a broad-spectrum antibiotic as a hedge, or "wait and see"

Point-of-care DNA sequencing flips that. That same swab could tell you:

  • Exactly which pathogen — not just "strep" but which strain
  • Whether it carries antibiotic resistance genes
  • Mixed infections — flu AND a secondary bacterial infection
  • Viral load — how far along are you
  • The thing nobody thought to test for

The hardware to do this already exists. Palm-sized. Real-time. Plugs into a laptop.

Hardware: $5,150. Flow cells: $400–800, one per patient run.

The bottlenecks are sample prep time and FDA clearance — not the sequencer.

Without it: doctors prescribe broad-spectrum antibiotics because they're flying blind. Broad-spectrum means broad damage — to your gut, to the population's resistance profile.

With it: targeted diagnosis, targeted treatment. Right drug, right dose, right pathogen.

We're 5–10 years from walk-in, answer-before-you-leave. But the trajectory is pointing straight at it.

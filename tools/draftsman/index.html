Draftsman

A free, open-source tool by Owen Corpening · First release January 2027

Draftsman takes a markdown draft to a publish-ready post. You talk to the AI app you already use, and Draftsman does the layout work: images found, licensed and credited, tables turned into figures, pull quotes picked and styled, figures numbered, weak lists fixed and a carousel built from the summary.

What it does

  • Images. It searches the image sources you turn on, passes a photo only when its license clearly allows commercial use and changes, saves it beside your article with its credit, and keeps a record of where it came from.
  • Tables. It turns a markdown table into a styled image with a numbered caption. If you sign in to Google, the table's data also goes into a new Google Sheet in your own Drive, and the image links to it.
  • Pull quotes, figures and lists. It styles pull quotes taken word for word from your article, keeps figure numbers in order, and finds lists that would read better as a table or a paragraph.
  • Carousels. It builds a LinkedIn-sized slide deck and PDF from the article's summary.

Your AI app suggests each change and you accept it or not. Every change can be undone.

How it runs

Draftsman is a local MCP server: it runs on your own computer, inside AI apps such as VS Code, Claude Code, Claude Desktop or Cursor. It calls no AI model of its own and has no server of its own. It sends nothing to me.

Google sign-in

Signing in to Google is optional and only needed to link table images to sheets. Draftsman asks for one permission, to see, edit, create and delete only the Google Drive files it creates, and nothing else in your Drive. Without it you get the table image alone.
